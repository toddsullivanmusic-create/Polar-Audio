# Lumen Audio — Market Analysis (November 2026)

> Purpose: decide which four plugins to build for maximum sellability in Logic Pro, and how to position them.
> Method: public retailer charts and editorial "best of" lists, KVR community threads, market-research category data, and a scan of the worship-production vendor space. Retailer sales figures are not public; where a claim is an inference it is marked.

## 1. What actually sells in the plugin market

**Category share.** Audio effects are roughly half of all plugin spending (≈48 % in 2025, growing ~6.6 %/yr). Within effects, the three evergreen categories by usage are EQ (~41 %), reverb (~33 %) and compression (~26 %); saturation / analog-emulation is the fastest-growing sub-category because it is the easiest to differentiate on *sound* rather than on features. Sources: market-research summaries (HTF, Dataintelo, MarketIntelo) — directional, not audited.

**What tops the charts.** The 2025 Plugin Boutique editorial "best to buy" list leads with FabFilter Pro-Q 4, then Antares Auto-Tune, Kontakt 8. The 2025 PB Awards best multi-FX was UA Ocean Way Studios. KVR's 2026 "best reverb" threads converge on Valhalla (VintageVerb / FutureVerb), Soundtoys SuperPlate, Relab LX480, FabFilter Pro-R 2. Saturation threads converge on Soundtoys Decapitator, FabFilter Saturn 2, Softube Saturation Knob, Wavesfactory Spectre.

**Why this matters for a new vendor.**

| Category | Market size | Incumbent moat | Differentiation on sound | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EQ | Largest | Very high (Pro-Q 4 is the default; 200+ clones) | Low — EQ is near-commodity | Avoid as a launch product |
| Compressor | Large | High (200+ vintage comp emulations on the market) | Medium | Later |
| **Reverb** | Large, high ASP ($99–$199) | Medium — Valhalla wins on price, FabFilter on UI; nobody owns *"gorgeous + modern + shimmer + ducking in one"* | **High** — people buy reverbs on taste, and keep buying new ones | **Build (AURA)** |
| **Saturation** | Fast-growing, $49–$149 | Medium — Decapitator is 2009-era UI, Saturn is complex | **High** — "vibe" products sell on demos and presets | **Build (GLOW)** |
| Synth | Large, $99–$199 | High for workstations; analog-poly niche is healthy (Arturia, u-he Diva, TAL) | High | Build as the "flagship instrument" (McGEE) |
| Worship / live playback | Niche but *underserved and loyal* | **Very low** — sold as MainStage templates, audio drone files and iPhone apps, not plugins | Very high | **Build (SANCTUARY)** — category creation |

## 2. The two money-makers: reverb + saturation

1. **Reverb** is the category where customers own five and still buy a sixth. Price tolerance is high (Pro-R 2 $199, SuperPlate $149, LX480 $149, Valhalla $50). The winning formula in 2025–26 reviews: *algorithmic, modulated, musical, with a colour/era switch, shimmer, and a modern resizable UI*. Ducking is a frequently requested feature that most reverbs lack. AURA ships all of that.
2. **Saturation** is the category with the best demo-to-sale conversion — one before/after on a vocal sells it. Incumbents are either dated (Decapitator) or over-complex (Saturn). The gap: a *premium-feeling, one-screen* saturator whose circuits are actually different from each other and whose "Focus" lets you choose *where* in the spectrum the colour lands. GLOW is built for that pitch.

## 3. Worship / CCM production: the gap in Logic Pro

What Bethel / Upper Room / Maverick City-style productions need on a Sunday:

- **Pads in every key, instantly switchable, with a seamless crossfade** when the worship leader changes key or goes spontaneous.
- **Latch / "set and forget"** — the keys player needs hands free for piano.
- **Swell** on one fader or the mod wheel; **slow fade-out** at the end of a song.
- **Tempo-aware movement** so the pad breathes with the click.
- Something that lives *inside Logic* (many churches run Logic/MainStage for tracks and don't want Ableton).

What is sold today (Coresound, That Worship Sound, Worship Start, Sunday Sounds, Infinite Worship Sounds, Worship Pads Pro): **audio files in 12 keys**, **MainStage templates** with latch hacks ("you'd need a third arm" is literally their copy), and **phone apps**. Recurring pain points in the copy: key changes are clunky, pads are 15-minute files that end, nothing is a real instrument plugin, nothing reacts to tempo. Logic itself has no "pad in a key" instrument.

**SANCTUARY** is a generated (not sampled) pad engine: infinite sustain, any key, major/minor, seamless key crossfade, MIDI key-switching from any keyboard, mod-wheel swell, one-button fade, bar-synced motion, AURA's reverb inside. It is the first product in the category that is an *instrument*, so it can be sold at a plugin price ($79–$99) rather than a pack price ($25–$40), and it creates obvious upsells (style packs, a "Song Map" set-list mode, a MainStage-ready concert).

## 4. The synth

The brief asked for a "McGee"-style analog synth. McGEE is positioned as the *vibe synth*: 3 PolyBLEP oscillators, a Moog-style 4-pole transistor-ladder model with drive, 16 voices, unison "Fat", chorus and delay built in, and worship-ready presets so it cross-sells into the SANCTUARY audience. Priced as a flagship instrument ($99–$129); it anchors the bundle.

Rationale: intro prices sit under the Valhalla/Soundtoys psychological ceilings, list prices signal "premium", the bundle is the real product (bundles are 40–60 % of revenue for most boutique vendors). Run a 14-day free trial (iLok-free, email-based licence) — trials are the strongest conversion lever for sound-driven products.
